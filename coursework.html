Coursework

This is a list of some classes I've taken at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign that I found really interesting, especially the ones that feed into risk modeling and macro analysis.

Current

ECON 490 — Political Economics

Institutions • Policy • Political Processes

How politics shapes economic outcomes: voting, lobbying, and institutions — the political forces behind economic policy.

ECON 460 — Financial Economics

Asset Pricing • Portfolio Theory • Market Efficiency

How financial markets price risk: portfolio theory, asset pricing models like the CAPM, market efficiency, and an introduction to derivatives.

ECON 437 — Game Theory

Nash Equilibrium • Strategy • Auctions

The mathematics of strategic interaction: Nash equilibrium, bargaining, and auction design — how rational players anticipate and respond to each other's moves.

Completed

ECON 491 — Applied Econometrics

Causal Inference • Panel Data • Program Evaluation

Empirical methods for answering causal questions with real data: panel data techniques, quasi-experimental designs, and program evaluation — the toolkit behind credible applied research.

ECON 469 — Economics of Risk

Uncertainty • Insurance • Decision Theory

How people and firms make decisions under uncertainty: expected utility, insurance markets, and the economic foundations of risk management.

STAT 425 — Statistical Modeling I

Linear Models • Regression Diagnostics • ANOVA

Theory and practice of linear statistical models: estimation, inference, regression diagnostics, and ANOVA — the foundation of applied modeling.

STAT 385 — Statistical Programming Methods in R

R • Data Wrangling • Visualization

Statistical computing in R: wrangling messy datasets, building clear visualizations, and implementing statistical methods from scratch.

Self-Study

FRM Part I — Financial Risk Manager (GARP)

Quantitative Analysis • Financial Markets • Valuation • Risk Models

Self-studying for the FRM Part I exam (November 2026): quantitative analysis, financial markets and products, valuation and risk models, and the foundations of risk management.

CFA Level I — Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA Institute)

Ethics • Quantitative Methods • Financial Reporting • Portfolio Management

Preparing for the CFA Level I exam: ethical and professional standards, quantitative methods, financial statement analysis, and the foundations of portfolio management.
